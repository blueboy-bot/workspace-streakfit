## Catalog-wide reasoning

The catalog generally uses task constraints rather than universal anatomical rules: comfortable range, controllable load, stable support, gradual tempo, and avoiding momentum. These are defensible coaching defaults. “背部中立,” “膝盖朝脚尖,” “肘部稳定,” and “不借力” are not, by themselves, claims that small movement variations cause injury. They should remain guidance, not be promoted to absolute visual pass/fail or injury guarantees.

Useful scientific guardrails already present include natural scapular upward rotation in `shoulderpress`; comfortable range/depth in multiple movements; controlled extension rather than banning joint lockout in `legpress`, `preacher`, and `shouldermachine`; explicit statements that grip does not isolate a muscle head in `barcurlWide`, `barcurlNarrow`, and `tricepsSupinated`; and that higher foot position does not remove quadriceps participation in `legpressHigh`.

`bandpallof` says not to convert band resistance to kilograms. Interpreted as a product instruction against uncalibrated load equivalence, this is appropriate. It should not be expanded into the scientific claim that band tension can never be measured at a specified elongation.

`tempobench` (“不憋气拖延”) and `cablecrunchStanding` (“不靠…屏息完成”) discourage prolonged or compensatory breath holding. They do not explicitly deny that a brief breath hold can accompany bracing; no categorical breathing myth is established. If these are expanded, distinguish comfortable breathing during light practice from an absolute prohibition applicable to all loading contexts.

`rowing` gives a useful abbreviated sequencing cue (“先蹬腿再拉手柄 / 回程先伸臂再屈膝”). It omits the intermediate trunk pivot, but does not contradict it. An expanded rowing guide should include the hip/trunk transition; the three-item cue set need not be rejected for being abbreviated.

`bridgeSingle` asking for a level pelvis and other instructions to avoid swaying/twisting describe control goals for these selected exercise variants. They are not proof that any small pelvic deviation is harmful. Similarly, pause/tempo prescriptions of 1–2 seconds or approximately 3 seconds define the variant; they are not universal optimal-training claims.
