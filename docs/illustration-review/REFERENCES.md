# STREAKFIT posture reference findings

Research performed 2026-10-15. Read-only source research; no application code was changed or published. This report supports a visual illustration audit, not medical advice or a biomechanical certification.

## Evidence actually read

The following PubMed **complete abstract texts were read in accessible search-result HTML**. Publisher full texts and videos were not read. Local evidence HTML is in `posture-reference-cache/` beside this report. The article URLs identify the publications; search URLs identify the accessible route actually read. Direct article access can behave differently.

1. **Saeterbakken AH, Fimland MS. “Effects of body position and loading modality on muscle activity and strength in shoulder presses.”** J Strength Cond Res. 2013;27(7):1824–1831. PMID 23096062. DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e318276b873.
   - Article: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/
   - Read: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Saeterbakken+shoulder+presses&format=abstract
   - Observed finding: 15 healthy men performed barbell/dumbbell shoulder presses seated and standing. Muscle activation and 1-RM differed between these configurations. Standing dumbbells had the greatest stability requirement and highest deltoid neuromuscular activity, but lowest 1-RM.
   - Illustration implication: identify the exact seated/standing and barbell/dumbbell variant before comparing posture. A reference for one variant does not validate every other variant.
   - Limit: this is an EMG/strength study, not an injury trial or a prescription for a universal trunk, elbow, or shoulder angle.

2. **Kim CR, Park DK, Lee ST, Ryu JS. “Electromyographic Changes in Trunk Muscles During Graded Lumbar Stabilization Exercises.”** PM R. 2016;8(10):979–989. PMID 27317910. DOI 10.1016/j.pmrj.2016.05.017.
   - Article: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27317910/
   - Read: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=bird-dog+lumbar+stabilisation+exercise&format=abstract&size=10
   - Observed finding: 10 healthy men performed curl-up, dead-bug, Superman, and bird-dog exercises at five intensity levels. Bird-dog progression increased multifidus and erector-spinae peak EMG. The authors advocate graded intensity according to tolerance and physical ability.
   - Illustration implication: distinguish starting quadruped, a single-limb progression, and the full bird-dog endpoint; these are different stages rather than interchangeable silhouettes.
   - Limit: small healthy male sample and surface EMG. The abstract supplies no universal joint-angle target or individual safety verdict.

3. **Dafkou K, Kellis E, Ellinoudis A, Sahinis C. “Lumbar Multifidus Muscle Thickness During Graded Quadruped and Prone Exercises.”** Int J Exerc Sci. 2021;14(7):101–112. PMID 34055178. DOI 10.70252/PVLQ9812.
   - Article: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34055178/
   - Read in the same bird-dog search above.
   - Observed finding: 21 recreational athletes performed graded Superman and bird-dog variants. The abstract explicitly describes the combined quadruped variant as **left upper limb plus right lower limb lift**, i.e. opposite arm and leg. Upper-limb, lower-limb, and combined tasks produced different multifidus thickness responses.
   - Illustration implication: a full bird dog should have contralateral reaching limbs and two remaining support limbs. A same-side arm/leg drawing should not be labeled the standard contralateral bird dog.
   - Limit: ultrasound contraction thickness is not a measurement of every joint's position, nor proof of a particular spinal curve or exact degree.

4. **Solstad TE et al. “A Comparison of Muscle Activation between Barbell Bench Press and Dumbbell Flyes in Resistance-Trained Males.”** J Sports Sci Med. 2020;19(4):645–651. PMID 33239937.
   - Article: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33239937/
   - Read in the shoulder-press search in item 1.
   - Observed finding: 17 resistance-trained men completed six-repetition-maximum bench press and dumbbell flyes. Muscle activation differed between the exercises and between descending/ascending phases. The fly is described as horizontal shoulder flexion with extended elbows.
   - Illustration implication: bench press and fly should remain visually distinct; a bent-elbow press cannot stand in for a fly, and an endpoint should be interpreted in its movement phase.
   - Limit: EMG is not joint-angle or injury evidence. This abstract does not justify a mandatory elbow angle, arch, or hand position for all bench press users.

## Practical limits for the illustration audit

- Use identifiable movement patterns, coherent limb connections, support contacts, and clearly specified exercise phase as the first review criteria. These are drawing-review criteria, not clinical measurements derived from the abstracts.
- No universal squat torso angle, squat depth, deadlift hip height, bench elbow angle, overhead-press elbow angle, row trunk lean, or hip-thrust knee angle was established by the sources read. Avoid presenting an exact degree as mandatory or scientifically certified.
- Match the exercise variant and equipment before reviewing its silhouette. A seated/standing or barbell/dumbbell variation can legitimately change posture and task demands. Clinical tolerance, physical ability, anatomy, mobility, and limb proportions require individual consideration; the sources read here do not quantify all of these factors.
- A stylized two-dimensional image loses depth, rotation, perspective, and some joint landmarks. It cannot establish true three-dimensional joint angles, bar path through time, balance, load distribution, muscle activation, pain, or injury risk. Independent visual review can detect obvious drawing errors; it cannot certify real movement kinematics or an individual's technique.
- For a hip hinge/deadlift, pulldown/row, hip thrust, and calf raise, **no movement-specific full abstract or authoritative coaching instructions were successfully read in this pass**. Do not claim these families were scientifically validated by this report. Their illustrations can still receive a clearly labeled visual plausibility review.
